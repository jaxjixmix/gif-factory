# Body-text writing effect

Accepted three-page trial: 18s total, real 6fps; after each page settles or the body text switches, a 1/6s delay, then two lines written in about 0.9s, with titles and page numbers static. Fast writing pairs with a soft scratch sound that stops when the writing stops, preserving the original reading and action durations. Starting values may be adjusted to copy length; never squeeze out reading time for the effect.

## Text

- Keep the user-accepted font, colour, size, position, line spacing and line breaks. A font is not stroke-order data; do not pass off a traced font outline as handwriting.
- Reveal the final font's alpha progressively with per-character, per-stroke paths; do not substitute a horizontal line wipe, typewriter character popping or a whole-group fade-in for writing.
- Standard Hanzi stroke-order centrelines may be mapped onto the actual font's ink bounding box, then used as a stroked reveal mask; state that this is an approximation and do not claim to restore the font's true writing trajectory. Punctuation without matching data may appear briefly; missing glyphs and body-text Hanzi must not be silently skipped.
- Cache glyph shapes and geometry and derive reveal progress deterministically from time. The completed final state must restore the full original glyphs; a mask that is too narrow must not leave leftover strokes. A font change requires re-matching the strokes and re-checking.
- Each body-text group gets an independent start time; with multiple dialogue groups on one page, re-write to each group's beat. Every start time and its writing sound derive from one shared timeline.
- During a page turn the new page's body text stays blank; it is written only after the page settles. Text already written on the old page turns away with it; do not write backwards or restart it during the turn.

## The scratch sound

Reuse the accepted writing asset, cut to a short sound the same length as the write-in. Remove it when the user requests silence; do not add narration, pen clicks or a constant noise floor. Keep it clearly below the music, easing in and out, preserving the music's continuity; the gap from finishing the writing to the next stroke must be quiet.

Accepted case: a 0.9s short sound, about 20ms fade-in and 50ms fade-out; asset peak about -20.9dBFS and about -44.3dBFS average over the whole track including gaps — for reproducing that asset only. A new source needs fresh measurement; do not mechanically copy the gain numbers. The sample asset comes from https://sfxmint.com/sounds/pencil-writing-01 , marked CC0 and AI-generated by the source site; do not call it a real recording. Standard Hanzi stroke-order samples come from https://github.com/skishore/makemeahanzi ; use and distribution follow the licence of the data actually used, and font, data and sound-effect licences are not one and the same.

The sound effect may be kept as an independent stem and mixed with the music, so volume stays adjustable. After multiple trim/delay outputs, measure first: an audio track in the file does not mean there is sound; a non-empty WAV with a fully silent track has already occurred. Check actual non-zero samples, level, each cue's start and end, total duration and the final mix peak. Without a human listen, do not claim the sound passes.

## Review

For each layout, spot-check four states: before the page settles, settled but unwritten, mid-write, and fully written; with multiple dialogue groups also check the switch. Confirm the final complete text matches the original, with no leftover strokes and no clipped glyphs. Real 6fps completes the write-in in only about 5–6 frames — have the user confirm this tempo; do not silently raise the frame rate. Expand to the full story only after acceptance, and re-check all body-text cues and sound gaps.
