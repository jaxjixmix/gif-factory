## What It Looks Like

The name, object and gradient share one glyph outline; readable text, handwritten pressure, weight shifts and clean stroke endings are kept. The upper stroke flow and the lower letterform sit physically apart. The nine cover crow, comet, moon arc, butterfly wing, ocean wave, deer antlers, leaf veins, lightning and sun disc; changing the object means redesigning the stroke flow, not just swapping the name.

## How It Moves

The upper stroke is drawn in a quick sweep, holds about a second, then the lower part is written fast and tapers off. Unwritten areas stay blank; no micro-shake, tail-tip rebound or metallic glow. Defaults: standalone MP4 960×640 at 60fps, GIF at 25fps, 4-second loop. Existing baseline media keeps the version accepted at the time; no promise that every piece restores the exact real writing order.

## How to Produce

Read [the production workflow](../../references/dynamic-signatures.md) and [the standalone skill](../../skills/dynamic-art-signature/SKILL.md). Layer by actual component; check the phase where the upper stroke is alone and where the letterform turns into the trail; don't fake underprint and repeated strokes with whole-image transparency drops or wide masks.

## Chinese Cursive and Three-Character Signatures

Cursive by default; when running script is explicitly requested, keep the full structure and cut down on connected strokes. Chinese also keeps a visible image, with the upper graphic and lower letterform physically apart; three-character names rearrange width, center of gravity and the long finisher on the last character, reusing the two visual stroke flows — no squeezing or dropping characters.
